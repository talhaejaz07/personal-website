Induction cooking can make sense in Pakistan, but it is not automatically the cheapest way to cook. Using October 2026 prices and a simple efficiency comparison, induction can beat LPG when your added electricity costs less than about Rs 56 per unit. Lower-slab piped gas can still cost much less per unit of useful heat.

That distinction matters. A cooker’s efficiency tells us how much energy reaches the pot; it does not tell us what that energy costs on your bill.

With conflict involving Iran and renewed attacks on commercial shipping near the Strait of Hormuz, energy supply risks are back in focus. The [Associated Press reported a rise in tanker incidents on October 22](https://apnews.com/article/iran-united-states-war-hormuz-trump-israel-2b126da67b09de2f328846eb656abe0e). That is a reason to think about household energy choices, but it does not make induction cheaper by itself.

## How I’m comparing cooking costs

I compare the cost of heat that reaches the cookware, rather than comparing a kilogram of gas directly with a kilowatt-hour of electricity. The figures below are estimates, not laboratory measurements of a particular Pakistani stove or pan.

[ENERGY STAR estimates that induction transfers about 85% of heating energy to cookware, compared with roughly one third for gas cooking products](https://www.energystar.gov/products/electric_cooking_products). Actual results vary with burner, pan size, cookware, and cooking method. I use 33% for both LPG and piped gas to keep this comparison simple.

## LPG: roughly Rs 66 per useful kWh

OGRA’s October 2026 LPG notification set the consumer price at Rs 279.34 per kg. An 11.8 kg domestic cylinder was priced at Rs 3,296.23. The [OGRA notification page](https://ogra.org.pk/index.php/lpg-notified-prices) lists the monthly prices; the October figure is also reported by [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2033865). Shop prices and delivery charges can be higher.

LPG’s lower heating value is about 44–46 MJ per kg, or roughly 12.2–12.8 kWh per kg, depending on its composition ([IEA Advanced Motor Fuels](https://iea-amf.org/content/fuel_information/lpg/fuel_properties)). Using 12.8 kWh and a 33% stove efficiency:

- Useful heat from 1 kg: 12.8 × 0.33 = **4.22 kWh**
- Fuel cost per useful kWh: Rs 279.34 ÷ 4.22 = **about Rs 66**

This excludes any delivery or handling charges and the time or effort involved in arranging a refill. For a personal comparison, use the amount on your actual refill receipt.

## Piped gas: the tariff slab changes the answer

Piped gas is not one flat-price fuel. [SSGC’s domestic tariff schedule](https://www.ssgc.com.pk/web/?page_id=103) lists protected rates from Rs 200 to Rs 350 per MMBtu and non-protected rates from Rs 500 to Rs 4,200, depending on monthly consumption. The government kept consumer tariffs unchanged for FY 2026–27, according to this [July 2026 report](https://www.geo.tv/latest/671871-govt-keeps-gas-prices-unchanged-despite-ogra-reduction).

Using 293 kWh per MMBtu and the same 33% stove-efficiency assumption, those tariff rates work out to roughly **Rs 2–43 per useful kWh of fuel**. For example, non-protected rates of Rs 500–1,250 per MMBtu work out to about **Rs 5–13 per useful kWh**, before fixed charges and taxes.

That range is wide because the applicable rate depends on the household’s category and monthly usage. Gas bills also include charges such as meter rent, fixed charges, and applicable taxes; [SSGC explains its billing components here](https://www.ssgc.com.pk/web/?page_id=111824). So the calculation is a comparison of fuel charges, not a prediction of anyone’s full monthly bill. If your piped gas is available and you fall in a lower tariff slab, it may remain the cheapest option on cost alone.

## Induction: compare it with your next electricity unit

Induction transfers about 85% of its electricity to the cookware. If the added electricity for cooking costs Rs 50 per kWh, then:

- Cost per useful kWh: Rs 50 ÷ 0.85 = **about Rs 59**

At those assumptions, induction is slightly cheaper than October LPG, but more expensive than lower-slab piped gas. Its LPG break-even point is about **Rs 56 per electricity unit**: below that, induction costs less per useful kWh than LPG; above it, LPG costs less on fuel alone.

Rs 50 is only an example, not a national electricity rate. Your actual cost depends on your tariff category, consumption, taxes, and bill adjustments. Check the extra cost of the units you expect to use for cooking; an average bill divided by total units can give a misleading answer when tariffs change by slab.

## Where induction has a stronger case

**It heats quickly and wastes less heat into the room.** Induction heats compatible cookware directly. ENERGY STAR says it can boil water nearly twice as fast as gas or conventional electric cooking and releases less heat into the kitchen.

**It avoids burning gas at the cooktop.** Gas cooking produces combustion pollutants such as nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, and formaldehyde. Induction has no flame or combustion at the cooking surface ([U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/articles/making-switch-induction-stoves-or-cooktops)). The glass can still become hot from the pan, so it is not a cold surface while cooking.

**It can use surplus solar electricity.** If your panels are producing more electricity than the home can use, cooking with that surplus can improve the value of the system. Solar does not make induction automatically free: stored electricity has conversion losses and battery wear, and electricity used for cooking may have another value.

**It is easier to clean and removes LPG cylinder logistics.** The flat surface is simple to wipe, and using induction avoids booking, storing, and replacing LPG cylinders. You do need compatible cookware: if a magnet sticks firmly to the bottom of a pot or pan, it will usually work.

There are trade-offs. Induction needs electricity, a suitable circuit or inverter, and cookware that works with it. During an outage, it cannot cook unless your backup system can handle the cooktop’s load. It also does not make every cooking task cheaper than gas.

## My conclusion

My original draft made induction sound like a generally cheaper and more reliable replacement. The updated comparison is more mixed. At October’s LPG price, induction can be competitive if your added electricity costs less than about Rs 56 per unit. For households on lower piped-gas slabs, gas is likely cheaper on fuel cost while it is available.

The case for induction is strongest for households relying on LPG, people with usable daytime solar surplus, and anyone who values faster cooking, less kitchen heat, easier cleaning, or avoiding indoor combustion pollution. For a home with steady low-slab piped gas, switching purely to save money may not pay off.

Induction is a useful option for Pakistan’s changing energy mix, but the right choice depends on your bill, your supply reliability, and how you cook. Before buying, check your electricity rate, gas slab, inverter capacity, and cookware.
