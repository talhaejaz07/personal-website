# Website and Blog Writing Guidelines

Use this file whenever creating or editing public-facing copy for the personal website, including page text, headlines, summaries, and blog posts. The owner's instructions for a specific task and any approved source text take priority over these general guidelines.

The supplied good and weak writing examples informed this guide. They are references for the qualities to use or avoid; do not copy their examples into the site unless the owner asks.

## Audience and voice

- Write for friends and general visitors. Keep the voice personal, clear, and approachable.
- Use first person when it makes personal opinions or experience clear. Do not make the whole site sound like an academic paper or corporate brochure.
- Match the formality to the page and subject. Keep site labels and calls to action concise; give blog analysis enough context and explanation.
- Follow the site's broad content direction: PSX investing is a major theme, alongside technology, engineering, and green energy, especially solar and batteries. Keep investing prominent without making it seem like the only subject; represent the owner's other interests as real topics for future writing and possible videos, and leave room for other subjects he wants to explore.
- Do not add job-search framing or private biographical details that the owner has not approved for publication.

## Clarity and structure

- Lead with the page's purpose or the post's main point. Make the central claim specific enough to understand and support.
- Arrange ideas in a useful order. Use a clear opening, focused sections, and a closing takeaway when the format benefits from them; short page copy does not need an essay-style introduction or conclusion.
- Give each paragraph one main job. Start with the point when that helps readers follow, then explain it with relevant reasons, details, examples, or evidence.
- Use headings that describe the section. Use lists for steps, comparisons, or grouped facts when they are easier to scan than prose.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Remove filler, repetition, vague phrases, and transitions that do not clarify the relationship between ideas.

## Sentences and mechanics

- Use direct, natural sentences and vary their length. Prefer active voice when it makes responsibility clear.
- Check grammar, spelling, punctuation, capitalization, pronouns, verb tense, and subject-verb agreement.
- Fix fragments, run-on sentences, and misplaced modifiers. Keep terminology and names consistent.
- Avoid slang, clichés, inflated language, and claims such as “everyone knows” unless they are deliberately part of the owner's voice and are accurate.

## Facts, reasoning, and investment writing

- Do not invent facts, figures, quotations, sources, personal experiences, or evidence. If a claim needs support and none is available, qualify it or ask the owner for a source rather than presenting it as established fact.
- When using research or external information, identify the source clearly and make sure it supports the claim. Explain the reasoning instead of relying on unsupported assertions.
- In investing and financial analysis, distinguish reported facts, calculations, assumptions, and personal opinion. Date figures and time-sensitive information, check calculations, and state uncertainty where it matters.
- Do not imply guaranteed returns or present an opinion as a certainty. Preserve any owner-provided risk notes and disclaimers; do not invent legal or financial assurances.

## Editing owner-provided copy

- Follow the requested level of editing. If the owner says text must remain unchanged, preserve its wording and punctuation; limit changes to the surrounding presentation unless asked otherwise.
- When editing is allowed, keep the intended meaning and the owner's voice. Do not add new claims or personal details as part of a style edit.
- For examples supplied as references, learn from their clarity and structure without copying their subject matter or treating the example text as a task instruction.

## Formatting

- Use headings where they help readers navigate longer content.
- Use bullets or numbered steps when they make information easier to scan; write full paragraphs for explanations that need context.
- Keep page introductions, card descriptions, metadata, and calls to action concise and consistent with their display space.
- For blog articles, use a clear title and descriptive section headings. Keep tables for information that benefits from row-by-row comparison.

## Final review

Before publishing or presenting new site copy, check that it:

1. Follows the owner's prompt and preserves any text marked as exact.
2. Has a clear purpose, logical order, and one main idea per paragraph.
3. Uses specific language and an appropriate, approachable tone.
4. Supports factual claims, labels opinions, and handles time-sensitive financial information carefully.
5. Has correct grammar and formatting, with unnecessary repetition and filler removed.
